Bernhardt precision sundial

A 3D-printable equatorial sundial with a shaped roller gnomon (Bernhardtsche Walze) that reads mean zone time to the minute, computed for your location.

1. Location

2. Time zone

Detected automatically from the location. The scale shows standard time all year, no daylight-saving shift: add one hour while summer time is in effect (this note is also engraved on the dial).

3. Size & options

The shape is scale-free, so any size works. Only the 8 mm plugs and the minimum roller neck (4 mm) stay fixed. Up to 150 mm fits a 180 mm bed; 5-minute ticks below 160 mm, minute ticks above.

Design

    How it works and how to use it

    Principle

    The dial is an equatorial ring: a scale circle perpendicular to the Earth's axis, with the gnomon on the axis. Instead of a thin rod the gnomon is a roller whose radius changes along its length. Each day the sun's declination selects a different height of the roller, and at that height the roller is exactly as thick as the equation of time demands for that day. The leading edge of the roller's shadow therefore lands on the mark of the current mean time, not the true solar time. Because every declination occurs twice a year with different equation-of-time values, there are two rollers, swapped at the solstices. The longitude correction of your zone is rotated into the scale.

    Reading

    Read where the leading shadow edge (the edge ahead in the direction the shadow travels) crosses the tick ends on the outer ring. The scale is standard time; add one hour during summer time. Near the solstices the roller profile flattens, so expect a couple of minutes uncertainty for about a week.

    Printing

    • dial.stl: scale side up as exported; enable tree supports for the wing undersides and the hub. 0.15–0.2 mm layers; the engraving is 0.6 mm deep.
    • roller_1.stl / roller_2.stl: pin down, as exported, with a brim. Roller I has one groove on the collar, roller II has two.
    • stand.stl: base down. Below 45° latitude the stem overhang is steep, so add supports or lay the stand on its fin.
    • Plugs are 8 mm with 0.15 mm clearance; sand lightly if tight.

    Setting up

    1. Level the base. Point the fin exactly to true north (true south on the southern hemisphere); a phone compass with declination correction or the noon shadow of a plumb line works.
    2. Put the dial on the stand pin; the flat on the pin keys the orientation.
    3. Insert the roller for the current half year, collar resting on the hub.
    Source: pinguinonice/parametric-sundial. Geometry after J. R. Oliver (1892) and M. Bernhardt (1966), see Glaeser & Hofmann, Über minutengenaue Sonnenuhren für die Mittlere Zeit (2004).
